In this world, your backpack weighs exactly as much as your WORRIES, and emptying it doesn't HELP.

My math teacher wheels hers in on a cart. During finals week, the school bus drags sparks down the road. Babies' diaper bags float. Parents tie them to the stroller like balloons. My grandma's purse weighs nothing. She says she ran out at seventy.

Mine FLOATS too. I'm fifteen, and I tie my backpack to my wrist so it won't blow away. Dad calls me "the kite." My little brother Benji is the happiest kid in fourth grade. Gap-tooth grin. Sings to the toaster. And every morning, he drags his bag to the bus with both hands.

The morning he left for Camp Wobbly Pine, his bag wouldn't MOVE at all. It had cracked his bedroom floor. The bus left at eight. It was 7:31. Benji kept grinning. "It's FINE! I'll just live here now."

I told him my best joke. He laughed for a full minute. The bag lost four ounces. So I emptied it. Two socks, a toothbrush, a frog flashlight. Still stuck. So I tied it to Dad's riding mower and hit the gas. The mower did a wheelie. The bag didn't budge. 7:48.

So I sat on the floor next to it. "Benji. What's in there?" He stopped grinning. "You wouldn't get it. Yours FLOATS." I untied my bag from my wrist and unzipped it. "Then put ONE in mine." He looked at me for a while. "Mom's car makes a noise." My bag dropped an inch. "Okay. That one's MINE now."

He kept going. Dad's job. The stove. If I'd pass math. If I packed my lunch. If I'd forget him when I got my license. Six worries. Half of them were about me. My bag hit the floor so hard the frog flashlight turned on. Benji picked his up with one hand. Mine had been floating for years because he was carrying my HALF.

He made the bus by nine seconds. My backpack weighs nineteen pounds now. I checked the stove twice today. Dad stopped calling me the kite. BEST backache I've ever had.
