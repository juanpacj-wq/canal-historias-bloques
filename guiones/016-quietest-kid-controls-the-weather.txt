In this school, the weather over the building follows the MOOD of the kid who talks the LEAST.

That kid is Tobin. He said eleven words all last year. Nine of them were "here." When he aced a spelling test, we got sun for a week. When he dropped his sandwich, it hailed on the parking lot. Our weather app is just a photo of his face. And every time a cloud moves, six hundred kids turn around and stare at him.

Me, I talk the MOST. Teachers have timed it. My record is nineteen minutes without a breath. So the sky has never once been my problem.

Then came Field Day week. It rained nine days straight. Principal Ruiz said if it rained Friday, Field Day became a three-hour assembly about fire exits. Everybody looked at Tobin. Then everybody looked at me. "I'll fix him," I said.

First, cupcakes. Fourteen of them, stacked on his desk. We got four minutes of sun. Then fog. Second, the marching band played his favorite song under his window. Hail, the size of meatballs. The tuba filled up. Third, I got six hundred kids in the gym chanting WE LOVE TOBIN. Lightning hit the flagpole. Twice. Tobin ran.

I found him under a library table. I opened my mouth. And for the first time in my life, I CLOSED it. I sat down next to him. I said NOTHING. Not that afternoon. Not all of Thursday. The nurse took my temperature. Tobin said "here" at attendance. I didn't. By noon, the rain stopped. The sky was mine now.

It wasn't sunny. It was fog. Thick, nervous fog, with one tiny cloud going in circles. Six hundred kids turned around and stared at me. Now they knew what the loud kid looks like on the inside. Tobin looked up at my fog. Then he said three words. "Oh. You TOO?" And he started talking. About frogs. For nineteen minutes. Nobody was watching him. The fog burned off.

Friday, I kept my mouth shut. Seventy-four and sunny. Tobin won the sack race, screaming the whole way. We take turns now. I've got the sky on Mondays. Bring a jacket.
