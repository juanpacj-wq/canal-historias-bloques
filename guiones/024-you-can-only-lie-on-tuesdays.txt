In this town, you can only lie on TUESDAYS. The other six days, only the TRUTH comes out.

On Monday my teacher said we're her least favorite class. On Sunday Grandma said my sweater makes me look like a couch. So everybody saves the fake stuff for Tuesday. Birthday presents get opened on Tuesdays, so you can say "I love it." Dentists only work Tuesdays. "This won't hurt a bit." The weatherman says it's raining tacos. But there's a catch. NOBODY believes one word you say on a Tuesday.

I keep a Tuesday List. Fourteen lies, ready to go. "I flossed." "Great meatloaf, Dad." And number fourteen. Tell Tess I like her. If she laughs, say "GOTCHA, it's Tuesday." I've done it thirty-one Tuesdays in a row. She laughs every time. Then every Wednesday she falls asleep in first period. Nobody knows why.

Last Monday, Tess told me her family is moving. Wednesday. Seven a.m. So I had one day left to say it. And that day was a Tuesday.

I tried to make a Tuesday count. First I just said it. "I like you. For REAL." She said, "Good one," and punched my arm. Then I wrote it on a card. She put it in a shoebox with nine other Tuesday cards. Then I went big. A forty-foot banner on the gym roof. TESS, I MEAN IT. Plus the whole drum line. She clapped. "Best Tuesday bit ever."

That night I stared at my list. And I got it. Six days a week, she would have had to believe me. I picked the one day I could take it back. Thirty-one times. So at 11:58, I stood under her window. No banner. No drums. I waited two minutes. Longest two minutes of my life. At 12:01, on a WEDNESDAY, I said, "I like you. And I can't say gotcha."

Her window was already open. She was already there. "FINALLY," she said. "I've stayed up past midnight thirty-one Tuesdays in a row. Just to see if you'd say it again on Wednesday." It was Wednesday. She couldn't be lying. So that's why she slept through first period.

She still moved at seven a.m. It's forty minutes away. I visit every Wednesday. I threw out the Tuesday List. Okay, I kept one. Great meatloaf, Dad.
