In this world, everyone can see who you MISS, because their silhouette walks right next to you.

It's gray and see-through, like a person made of fog. My mom's is shaped like my grandma. My gym teacher's is shaped like his own hair in 2009. The more you miss somebody, the sharper it gets. It only goes away when you see them again, in person. So nobody gets to play it cool. My sister told everyone she was "so over" Kyle. Kyle's fog was carrying her backpack.

Mine is shaped like Barty Finch. My biggest RIVAL. Three years of war. Spelling bees. One corn-eating contest that sent us both to the nurse. Then he transferred to a school six miles away. Now I win everything. Last week I won the spelling bee with the word "cat." Also, some guy in a fake mustache keeps booing me from the back row. Unrelated.

Two days after he left, foggy Barty showed up beside me. Everyone saw. And yearbook photos are Friday. I am not getting photographed next to my rival. He's getting sharper, too. He has EYEBROWS now.

So I tried to get rid of him. First, I tried missing someone else harder. I stared at pictures of my grandma for two hours. Now I had TWO fogs. They high-fived. Second, I put a wig on him and said he was my cousin. The wig fell straight through. Third, I held auditions for a new rival. Seven kids showed up. The best one lost on purpose and said "good game." DISGUSTING. Foggy Barty got so sharp I could count his freckles. Eleven.

So Thursday I biked six miles to his school. I waited at the gate. When he came out, I just said it. "I win everything now, and it feels like nothing. Nobody else argues right. I MISS you, okay?" My fog POPPED like a bubble.

Then I noticed. There was nothing next to Barty. NO fog. My stomach dropped. He turned red. He reached into his backpack and pulled out a fake mustache. "You can't miss somebody you see every week," he said. "Also, your spelling is embarrassing." He'd come to every contest. Six miles each way. Just to BOO me.

He transferred back on Monday. We're tied, two hundred twelve to two hundred twelve. Nobody walks next to me anymore. Except Barty. He still boos.
