In this world, your heartbeat plays out loud as MUSIC, and everybody near you can hear it.

My dad's heart plays banjo. When the electric bill comes, it plays banjo, but faster. My mom's heart plays trumpet. You can hear her getting mad from the driveway. Our neighbor Mr. Lutz says he is totally calm. His heart plays one long CAR ALARM. And it never turns off. This town has not been quiet for one second, ever.

Except me. My heart beats fine. It just doesn't play. The doctor listened for forty minutes and wrote "WEIRD" on my chart. Kids at school call me Mute Button. My only fan is our cat, Colonel Waffles. He sleeps on my chest every night. I figured it was the hoodie.

Then my baby sister Pim was born. Her heart plays a tiny xylophone, extremely fast. She has not slept in NINE days. Nobody has. Mom put salt in her coffee. Dad said goodnight to the pantry. And Grandma arrives Friday. Her heart plays bagpipes.

Babies are supposed to fall asleep to a heart song. Banjo failed. Trumpet really failed. I had no song, so I faked one. First, I hid a speaker in my shirt. It played a lullaby for eleven seconds, then an ad for discount mattresses. Pim screamed. Second, I taped four music boxes to my chest. Four different songs at once. Pim screamed louder. Third, I paid my cousin Bruno six dollars to hide behind me with a trombone. Pim screamed, the cat left, and a neighbor called the fire department.

At three a.m. it was my turn again. No speaker. No Bruno. I picked her up and told her the truth. "Sorry, Pim. You got the BROKEN one. Nothing plays in here." I held her against my chest anyway.

The xylophone slowed down. Then slower. Then soft, like rain on a window. Four seconds later, she was ASLEEP. I didn't move. In a house full of banjo and trumpet, I was the only QUIET place she'd ever been. Colonel Waffles climbed up next to her and gave me a look. He'd known for years.

Now everybody naps on me. Mom. Dad. Bruno, once. Grandma's bagpipes are booked for Tuesday. Kids still call me Mute Button. Their parents pay me nine dollars an hour to be quiet.
