In this world, your SHADOW walks toward whoever you like, and you can't call it BACK.

It just gets up and goes. My brother's shadow followed a lifeguard around for a whole summer. My math teacher's shadow lies across the lunch lady's shoes every day. They both pretend not to notice. It's not only people, either. My uncle's shadow has been parked outside the donut shop since 2011. Noon is safe, because shadows are tiny. Sunset is dangerous. At sunset, your shadow is FORTY feet long.

Mine likes Juniper Vale. She sits two rows ahead of me in science. She carries a yellow umbrella everywhere, even when it's sunny. Everyone says she's hiding her shadow from somebody. Probably Dax. Dax can do a backflip.

Then the school announced the Sunset Fair. Friday. On the soccer field. At six-thirty, when every shadow is forty feet long. Mine would cross the whole field and tap her on the shoulder in front of three hundred people.

So I tried to stop it. First, I tied my shadow to my ankle with a jump rope. It dragged me into a trash can. Second, I wore a sombrero the size of a kiddie pool. My shadow turned into a big circle and rolled toward her like a pancake. Third, at the fair, I pointed nine flashlights at myself to erase it. Instead, I got NINE shadows. All nine ran straight to Juniper. One of them was WAVING.

Three hundred people stared. So I did the only thing left. I walked across the field, right behind my own shadows. "Yeah. Those are mine. All nine. I LIKE you. Sorry about the waving one."

Juniper closed her umbrella. First time ever. Her shadow stood up, stretched, and walked right past me. It went straight to the nacho stand and hugged the cheese pump. "It's been like that since third grade," she said. "My shadow likes NACHOS. I get to PICK for myself." Then she took my hand.

We split a large. Dax did a backflip. Nobody looked. Her shadow still lives at the nacho stand. Mine brings it napkins.
