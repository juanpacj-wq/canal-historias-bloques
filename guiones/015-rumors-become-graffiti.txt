In this school, rumors paint themselves on the walls, and only come off when somebody says the truth out loud.

The sixth-grade hall once said MR. PELLMAN WEARS A WIG, in green letters two feet tall. He walked up and said, "Yes. His name is Stanley." Gone in one second. The cafeteria wall says THE NUGGETS ARE MOSTLY CARROTS. The lunch lady refuses to comment. It's been up four years.

I've never been on a wall. Not one letter. Nine years in this school, and nobody has ever said a word about me. I told myself that was a good thing.

Then Soren showed up. New kid. He could juggle oranges. By Friday, everybody knew his name. So at lunch I said, "I heard he got kicked out of his old school." I made it up. Monday morning, it was on the gym wall. Red letters, six feet tall. Soren stood under it, scrubbing with his sleeve.

I tried to fix it quietly. Tuesday, I used two buckets of soap. The letters just got shinier. Wednesday, I taped a bake sale poster over it. The letters crawled onto the poster. BAKE SALE: SOREN GOT KICKED OUT. Thursday at six a.m., I stood alone in the gym and whispered, "It isn't true." One letter faded. Then it all came back, bigger, and spread across the ceiling. The wall doesn't take half a truth.

Friday, the gym was full. Pep rally. Four hundred kids. I took the microphone from the mascot. "I made it up," I said. My voice shook. "Soren's been here one week and everybody likes him. I've been here nine years, and nobody even knows my middle name."

The letters peeled off like old tape. The wall went white. Nobody clapped. Soren looked at me for a long time. Then he walked out. Monday, there was new writing over my locker. Small. Blue. HENRIK'S MIDDLE NAME IS PROBABLY SOMETHING COOL. LIKE WOLFGANG. Soren walked past, juggling one orange. He didn't look at me. He was smiling.

It's Eugene. I am never saying that out loud. It's the only rumor in this school nobody wants to erase.
