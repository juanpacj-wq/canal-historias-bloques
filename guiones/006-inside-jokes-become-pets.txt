In this town, every inside joke turns into a PET, and it lives as long as you both keep laughing.

My parents have a tortoise from a joke about a waiter in 1999. Nobody remembers the joke. They just point at the tortoise and wheeze. The twins next door have fifty-two hamsters. My principal has zero pets and a very clean office. You can't fake it, either. A fake laugh makes them sneeze. And when one of you stops laughing for real, the pet goes SEE-THROUGH. Then it's gone.

Me and Ozzie had fourteen. The first one was Soup. Soup is a duck who wears one sock. He showed up on the worst day of my life. I dropped my lunch tray in front of the whole cafeteria. Ozzie looked at me. Then he dropped his on purpose. Boom. DUCK.

Then Ozzie's mom got a job nine hundred miles away. He moved in August. By October we were down to SIX. The llama went see-through in the middle of my math test. Turns out jokes don't TRAVEL well.

So I tried to save them. First, video calls. We laughed on a two-second delay. The hedgehog flickered like bad wifi and blinked out. Second, I mailed him forty-one pages of our best jokes. It took six days. By then the iguana was just a tail. Third, I recorded us laughing and played it for Soup all night on a loop. Soup sneezed two hundred times. By morning I could see my carpet through him.

Soup was the last one. So I called Ozzie, and I stopped doing bits. "I'm not laughing because it's funny anymore. I'm laughing so they don't disappear. If Soup goes, I think we go too." Ozzie was quiet. Then he said, "I've been FAKE laughing since Halloween."

Soup quacked once and faded out. Only the sock stayed. We both cried, on a two-second delay, so we had to take turns. Ozzie said we sounded like two broken tea kettles. And we lost it. REAL laughing. The can't-breathe kind. Something landed on my desk. A small, soggy pigeon with a face like it had been crying.

We named him Long Distance. He sleeps on Soup's sock. He only eats when Ozzie calls. He is getting EXTREMELY fat.
