In this world, you can LEND your courage to a friend, but you have NONE until they give it back.

It pops out of your chest like a warm orange marble. Your friend presses it to their chest, and it sinks right in. Now they're brave, and you're not. My dad lent his to my uncle for a wedding toast. Dad spent the whole party in the coat closet. Kids at my school rent theirs out. Two dollars a recess.

I never lent mine. Then my best friend Bram had to sing at the talent show. Bram is scared of dogs, escalators, and the word "volunteer." So I handed mine over. He dropped it in his shirt pocket and buttoned it. TWICE. He sang. He got a standing ovation.

That was three weeks ago. He never gave it back. Now Bram eats at the loud table by the window, and I'm scared of everything. Stairs. Soup. SOUP is hot, and it moves. And here's the problem. Asking for your courage back takes COURAGE.

So I wrote him a note. Four drafts. Then I got scared of his locker and ate it. I paid my little sister five dollars to ask for me. She asked for his pudding instead. She got it. Then I rented courage from a first grader named Zeb. Two dollars. It was the size of a pea. It was exactly enough to pet one goose. The goose bit me. Zeb cried until I gave it back. He kept the two dollars.

On Friday I walked to Bram's table with NOTHING. My knees knocked so loud that people turned around. I said, "I need it back. I'm scared of stairs. I'm scared of soup. And I'm scared you like this table better than me."

Bram unbuttoned his pocket. Twice. There it was. Still warm. He never USED it. "I was too scared to put it in," he said. "What if I broke it? So I just sang SCARED." Then why keep it? His ears went red. "It felt like you were up there with me." He looked at my knees. "Wait. You walked over here WITHOUT it?"

I pressed it back in. Stairs are just stairs again. Bram still borrows it every Friday. He never uses it. He just buttons it twice. And my uncle never returned Dad's. We slide pizza under the closet door.
