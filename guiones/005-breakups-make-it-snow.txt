In this town, every breakup makes it SNOW, but only over the house of whoever got dumped.

Small breakup, a few flakes. Big breakup, a BLIZZARD. When the mayor's wife left, he got six feet in July. When Mr. Dobbs got dumped by text, it snowed inside his car. Kids here keep sleds by the front door. We root against every couple. And the snow only stops when you're really over it. You can't fake it. The sky CHECKS.

It has been snowing on my house for three months. My brother Nico got dumped by Marisol in March. Neighbors still bring him soup. But Nico got over her in a week. He's dating Tabitha from the bowling alley. The snow is all on MY side of the roof. See, Marisol taught me to skateboard. She gave me my helmet. And nobody asks the LITTLE BROTHER.

Yesterday the roof made a sound like a giant chewing ice. Dad measured nine feet. The city said stop the snow by Saturday, or we move out. Everybody looked at Nico. So I had to get over her. Fast.

First, I just said it. "I'm over it." The cloud dropped one snowball on my head. It AIMED. Second, I boxed up everything she gave me. The helmet. The wheels. A birthday card with a skull on it. Three feet fell in one hour. The mailman quit. Third, I tried being mad at her. I wrote a list of everything wrong with Marisol. It had one thing on it. "LEFT." It snowed inside my sock drawer.

So I grabbed my board and skated to her house. Eleven blocks. A little trail of snow followed me the whole way. I knocked. Marisol opened the door. And I said the dumbest sentence of my life. "Did you break up with ME, too?"

She didn't answer. She pointed at her driveway. One TINY cloud. Snowing on one spot. The curb where she taught me to ollie. "Three months," she said. "I shovel that curb every morning." We just stood there. Then her little cloud thinned out. Eleven blocks away, so did mine.

We skate every Saturday now. Our lawn is green again. Nico is still dating Tabitha. I keep a sled by the door. I'm predicting two inches.
