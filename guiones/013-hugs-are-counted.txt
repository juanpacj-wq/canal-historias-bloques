In this world, you're born with exactly one THOUSAND hugs, and there's a counter on your wrist.

Every hug, it ticks down. But only for whoever opens their arms FIRST. So nobody goes first. My teacher only gives thumbs-ups. At our family reunion, forty people stood around with their arms down for six hours. My uncle cracked at his own wedding. He hit ZERO before the cake. Now he does finger guns. Dogs don't have counters. Nobody knows why. Everybody knows why.

I'm a spender. I always go first. When I was five, I used eleven hugs on one snowman. His name was Gerald. I'm fourteen now, and I have FOUR left. My grandma has nine hundred thirty-one. She has never opened her arms. Not once. She just pats your head twice, like she's checking a melon. I figured she didn't like hugs.

Four hugs. For the rest of my life. And Grandma turns eighty-one on Sunday. I wanted to give her one. I also wanted to keep it. So I tried to cheat the counter.

I hugged my mom wearing oven mitts. It counted. THREE. I hugged a tree and waited for it to go first. Trees never go first. TWO. Then my friend Hollis and I counted down and opened at the exact same time. It charged us both. ONE. Hollis is still mad.

On Sunday, I had one hug and one grandma. She sat in her chair with her hands folded. Same as always. I looked at my wrist. Then I decided she was worth it. I walked up and opened my arms.

She grabbed my wrists. "Put those away," she said. Then she opened hers. WIDE. I'd never seen her do that. Her counter ticked. Nine hundred thirty. Mine still said one. She smelled like peppermint. "Eighty-one years," she said. "I never went first. I was SAVING them. I just didn't know who for. Then I watched a five-year-old hug a snowman eleven times." She's hugged me every morning since. She always goes FIRST. Her number drops every day. Neither of us looks at it.

I still have my one. I'm saving it for something huge. Or for Gerald, if it snows. Grandma says that's the dumbest plan she's ever heard. She's knitting him a scarf.
