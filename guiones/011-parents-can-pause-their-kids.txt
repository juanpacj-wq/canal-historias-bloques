In this world, every parent gets TEN pauses, and one click FREEZES their kid for five whole minutes.

The kid doesn't feel a thing. You blink, and the clock jumped. Mr. Abernathy paused his twins in the middle of a pillow fight. They hung in the air like laundry. My friend Lupe's dad used all ten on one road trip. Lupe says it was the shortest drive of her life. And once they're gone, they're gone.

My mom had all ten. She NEVER used one. Not when I painted the dog green. Not when I flooded the bathroom to make a lake. Her clicker is still in the plastic, on her keys. I figured I was just an easy kid. Mom works at the diner all morning and the hospital laundry all night. She falls asleep standing up. Once she did it holding a spatula.

Pauses expire the day you turn fourteen. My birthday was Saturday. And a pause is the only break a parent ever gets. On Monday, Mom fell asleep in the middle of saying my name. So I made a plan. I'd be so annoying she'd have to click.

I played the recorder at six a.m. One song. Forty minutes. She hummed along. I filled the kitchen with two hundred balloons. She popped one, laughed, and made eggs. Then I invited my whole class to dinner without asking. Twenty-six kids. She made twenty-six grilled cheeses. There wasn't one left for her.

Friday night, 11:40, she came home. I tore the plastic off the clicker and put it in her hand. "PAUSE me," I said. "Please. Sit down for five minutes. I won't even know."

She laughed. Then her eyes got wet. "You think I'm tired because of you? You were NEVER easy. You're the only part of the day I'm AWAKE for." She sat down next to me. At midnight, the clicker went DARK. Mom fell asleep on my shoulder halfway through "happy birthday." So I didn't move. Not for my leg cramp. Not for the itch on my nose. Two hours and ten minutes.

Turns out I can pause MYSELF. I'm fourteen now. Nobody can freeze me. And my left arm still hasn't forgiven me.
